Display Ads - Creative studio

January 2026 - April 2026

  • UX Design
  • UX Research
  • Figma

Enabling our vendors to create display ad banners themselves, without help from an account manager or an external agency.

Role
Product Designer
Platform
Web & Mobile Web
Collaborators
4 Engineers, 1 Product designer, 1 Product manager
Contributions
Product design, Research, Information architecture, Interaction design and prototyping

Goal

Delivery Hero wanted more vendors to set up ads and promotions themselves through the vendor portal. Adoption was going well overall, but one ad type lagged behind: Display (banner) ads. Vendors, especially smaller ones, had no way to create banners that met the specs, so they left it to their account managers instead.

Outcome

We built a creative studio into the existing display booking flow, where vendors create banners themselves by adding their text and product images. They pick from multiple templates and only need to fill in their banner details. Within the launch region, the number of vendors booking display ads themselves each month grew from 10 to 45–50.

Why are banner ads important?

Banner ads are one of the quickest and easiest ways to build awareness in an app. They’re also versatile: vendors can use them to announce new menus, new deals and much more. On the business side, display is a premium ad type that drives revenue, which is why increasing its adoption was a priority.

Gathering context

Before tackling the problem, the team and I gathered as much context as we could on how to improve self-service booking of Display ads, speaking to both account managers and vendors. With account managers, we wanted to understand how they currently book display ads, from beginning to end. With vendors, we wanted to know what it would take for them to book these ads themselves, so their account managers could focus on more important work.

“I want to set up banner ads, but I don’t know how to create the banners, so I just trust my account manager to do it.”

Our early workshops and research showed that large vendors rarely worry about banner creation because they have in-house teams to make their banners, while smaller vendors rely heavily on their account managers or avoid display ads altogether.

From account managers, the overwhelming feedback was that although creating banners isn’t hard for them, they would rather spend that time on more important things, and some vendors are slow to respond when asked for feedback on how their product images look in the banners.

Building on the workshops, I went through existing research data from vendors and account managers and listed the pain points we could tackle directly.

Improve vendor experience

  • Create banners in the booking flow: Vendors create the banner during the flow and attach it straight to the campaign
  • Banner templates: Vendors can choose from a range of banner templates
  • Product images from the menu: Since this all happens in the vendor portal, vendors can pull in product images they’ve already uploaded to their menu instead of uploading new ones
  • Create banners with AI: Generate banners with AI directly in the vendor portal, then make a few tweaks

Reduce grunt work for AMs

  • Review banners with the campaign: Account managers only want to review the whole campaign, banners included, once everything has been created and sent for approval. This speeds up their work and gets campaigns live sooner
  • Less convincing needed: With an in-house tool for creating banners, account managers spend less time persuading vendors, especially smaller ones, to try display ads.

The solution

After the workshops and research, the solution was straightforward: we added a new CTA to the display ads flow that opens a creative studio.

What was there initially

  • A CTA that let vendors upload a ready-made banner to the campaign, following the banner guidelines (file size, width, height etc.), and send it for approval

What we added

  • A CTA that opens a creative studio, where vendors make banners from scratch using any template they like. Once they save, the banner becomes part of the campaign immediately.

Key findings & results

While building this, we realized our preferred final version, an AI creative studio that creates everything for the vendor from their menu items so they only need to edit the text, would take time to ship. As a stopgap, we launched this first version, where vendors upload their own product images and logo and fill in the rest. It all happens within the booking flow, so it’s still seamless and straightforward for vendors.

Version 2, which adds AI-generated banners, is currently in progress.

Since we launched version 1, self-booking of display ads has grown more than fourfold. In the region where we launched it, an average of 45–50 vendors now book display ads themselves every month, up from 10 before. We expect adoption to grow even further once the final version launches and vendors can create banners with AI.

Conclusion

This was a very important feature for Delivery Hero. Leading it, talking to real users and getting their feedback helped us reach our goal, and I learnt a lot about how these businesses approach creating ads.
